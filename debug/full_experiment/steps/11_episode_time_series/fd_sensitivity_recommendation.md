# Bounded FD sensitivity: diagnostic interpretation

The two bounds are exploratory assumptions. These calculations hold fitted discharge, duration and delay fixed; they are not a new calibration.

- expanded_lower_wave, balanced_four_pollutants: 137/137 inverse speeds are exactly attainable; 46 also preserve baseline capacity. 0 require the emission objective to be reoptimized within the bounds; 0 have no physical bounded pair.
- expanded_lower_wave, co2_target: 137/137 inverse speeds are exactly attainable; 48 also preserve baseline capacity. 0 require the emission objective to be reoptimized within the bounds; 0 have no physical bounded pair.
- primary, balanced_four_pollutants: 135/137 inverse speeds are exactly attainable; 44 also preserve baseline capacity. 2 require the emission objective to be reoptimized within the bounds; 0 have no physical bounded pair.
- primary, co2_target: 135/137 inverse speeds are exactly attainable; 46 also preserve baseline capacity. 2 require the emission objective to be reoptimized within the bounds; 0 have no physical bounded pair.

Most target speeds fit the exploratory box, but only 46 CO2-target cases and 44 balanced cases preserve the original capacity. The largest absolute capacity change is 314.4% for the CO2 target versus 26.2% for the balanced objective. Feasible FD values therefore do not by themselves establish a defensible permanent correction.

Use the primary-versus-expanded comparison to assess sensitivity to the lower wave-speed bound. Compare capacity changes, the retained traffic fit and pollutant-specific residuals before considering a new FD. If the proposal changes implied capacity, a subsequent corridor-level joint calibration and validation is needed before altering the production FD. Per-episode inverse pairs are explanatory diagnostics, not recommended per-episode parameters.
