# Arizona detector 141: November worked example

Open the [seven-page calculation report](reports/AZ_I10_Detector141_Emission_Diagnostic.pdf). It follows the detector-78 report format, including time-dependent VSP profiles and a separate rate-mapping diagnostic. This case belongs to the retained 137-episode comparison.

The episode is **I-10 westbound, detector 141, November 2016 average weekday, 15:45–19:05**. The archived monthly profile averages 13 complete weekdays; its `2016-11-01` date is a month label. Forty five-minute bins enter the episode calculation.

## Why this case

We required absolute errors of at most 5% in mean trip time and at most 10% in mean delay, peak delay, episode duration and the FD-derived queue speed. The observed-derived finite-link check must also pass. Four of the 137 episodes meet all conditions. Among those four, this case has the largest CO₂ absolute-error gap between two-speed lookup M and observed mean-speed S.

| Traffic quantity | Signed fitted-minus-observed difference |
|---|---:|
| Mean trip time | −4.382% |
| Mean delay | −8.682% |
| Peak delay | +5.258% |
| Episode duration | −1.831% |
| FD-derived queue speed | +5.065% |

CO₂ absolute differences from the observed-speed lookup proxy are **39.738% for M** and **7.973% for S**. This illustrates a substantial emission-representation difference when the selected traffic errors are small. It is a selected diagnostic case, not an unbiased estimate of overall method performance. The observed-derived queue speed is inferred from volume and duration, not a measured vehicle trajectory speed.

## VSP profiles on the common clock

Page 4 plots **operating-state VSP weighted by vehicle-hours**, on the same five-minute clock as the speed profiles. [VSP time series](results/vsp_time_series.csv) supplies 40 bins × five methods, and [VSP exposure reconciliation](results/vsp_exposure_reconciliation.csv) checks that the aligned profile preserves each method's total VSP-times-vehicle-hours.

Let $a_{ij}$ be the count from model node $j$ assigned to observed bin $i$, $T_j$ its whole-link travel time, and $z(v)$ the zero-acceleration VSP function. Then

$$\bar z_{R,i}=\frac{\sum_j a_{ij}T_jz(v_{R,j})}{H_{R,i}},\qquad
\bar z_{M,i}=\bar z_{A,i}=\frac{\sum_j a_{ij}[t_{f,j}z(v_f)+t_{q,j}z(v_q)]}{H_{R,i}}.$$

O uses $z(v_i)$; S uses the constant VSP at its observed harmonic mean speed. M and A overlap because their operating speeds and times are identical. The figure labels the shared curve **M = A**. Their emission rates remain different.

`vsp_exposure_weighted_kw_t` is the plotted quantity. `vsp_at_equivalent_speed_kw_t` is the separate diagnostic $z(L/\bar T_i)$; it is generally different. Neither the displayed VSP average nor the equivalent speed is substituted into the original M/A emission calculation. All 20 emission totals are preserved.

## Interpret the comparison correctly

- O is the observed five-minute detector speed used as a whole-link trip-speed proxy with an assigned emission rate. There are no observed vehicle trajectories or measured exhaust emissions for this case.
- S preserves O’s total vehicle-hours. R/M/A preserve the fitted vehicle-hours. R evaluates rates at reconstructed whole-link average speeds; M/A use free-flow and queue operating speeds.
- Common-clock plots allocate modeled vehicle groups to observed count groups in cumulative-count order. They conserve original totals; they do not estimate when emissions were physically released on the road.
- VSP is evaluated with zero acceleration and grade. The VSP at an equivalent whole-link speed is a diagnostic mapping and is not the operating-state VSP used internally by M/A. Their state distribution is shown separately.
- The signed error decomposition depends on the stated calculation order. It is arithmetic attribution, not a causal estimate of real emission error.

## Reproduce

From the repository root, with Python, NumPy, pandas and Matplotlib:

```sh
python debug/case_studies/az_i10_detector141_november/scripts/build_case_study.py
```

To compile the report as well, install `pdflatex` and run the same command with `--pdf`. An optional `--pdflatex PATH` locates the compiler. TeX package requirements match the detector-78 report. New outputs go to `debug/output/az141_november/`; published inputs and results are protected. The script verifies the selection and every saved emission total before producing the report. It does not recalibrate or change any manuscript result.
